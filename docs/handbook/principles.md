# ENGINEERING PRINCIPLES

> **Establish the reference.**
> **Build with intention.**
> **Leave the workshop better than you found it.**

---

`Good engineering is a collection of deliberate decisions.`

These principles are derived from the Workshop Inscription.

They exist to guide engineering judgment whenever certainty disappears.

They are not rules intended to restrict creativity.

They exist to encourage clarity, consistency, and thoughtful decision-making.

Whenever uncertainty arises, these principles should help guide the next decision.

---

## I. ESTABLISH THE REFERENCE

Good engineering begins with understanding.

Before implementation, we seek clarity.

Before change, we seek evidence.

Before decisions, we establish a reliable reference.

## 1. CLARITY OVER CLEVERNESS

Readable solutions are preferred over clever ones.

Code should communicate its intent before demonstrating technical sophistication.

When a simpler solution communicates the same idea, it should usually be preferred.

---

## 2. PREFER VERIFIED UNDERSTANDING OVER ASSUMPTIONS

Engineering decisions should be based on evidence whenever possible.

Documentation, implementation, and system behavior should be verified before introducing change.

Understanding the system is always preferable to making assumptions about it.

---

## 3. ARCHITECTURE FIRST

Implementation follows architecture.

Before writing code, responsibilities should be identified, interfaces should be understood, and the overall design should be considered.

Good architecture makes implementation simpler.

---

## II. BUILD WITH INTENTION

Every addition should have a purpose.

Every component should have a responsibility.

Every decision should be explainable.

Intentional systems remain understandable as they grow.

## 4. EVERY COMPONENT HAS ONE RESPONSIBILITY

Every file, module, and document should have a single, well-defined purpose.

Clear responsibilities make systems easier to understand, maintain, and evolve.

---

## 5. DOCUMENTATION IS PART OF THE PRODUCT

Documentation is not an afterthought.

Architectural decisions, engineering principles, and important lessons should be documented alongside the implementation.

Knowledge should be preserved, not assumed.

---

## 6. EVERY DEPENDENCY MUST JUSTIFY ITS PLACE

Dependencies should solve meaningful problems.

Features should never be added simply because they are popular or available.

Every dependency increases the responsibility of maintaining the project and should provide value that justifies its inclusion.

---

## 7. GROW DELIBERATELY

Features should be introduced intentionally.

The project values thoughtful evolution over rapid expansion.

Not every good idea belongs in Perpend.

---

## III. LEAVE THE WORKSHOP BETTER THAN YOU FOUND IT

Software should continue to teach long after it is written.

Every contribution should improve not only the project, but also the understanding of those who build it.

## 8. BUILD SOFTWARE THAT TEACHES

Every part of Perpend should help explain how and why it works.

The project exists not only to solve problems, but also to share the reasoning behind its solutions.

Understanding is a feature.

---

## 9. OPTIMIZE FOR THE NEXT READER

Code is read more often than it is written.

Decisions should favor the engineer who will read, maintain, or extend the project in the future.

Maintainability is a long-term investment.

---

## 10. CONSISTENCY COMPOUNDS OVER TIME

Small, consistent decisions create coherent systems.

Naming, structure, organization, and conventions should remain predictable throughout the project.

Consistency reduces cognitive load and improves long-term maintainability.

---

## A LIVING CONSTITUTION

These principles are intended to evolve alongside the project.

They are not preserved because they are old.

They are preserved because they continue to lead to better engineering.

If experience teaches us a better principle, Perpend should evolve accordingly.

Our commitment is not to tradition.

Our commitment is to understanding.
